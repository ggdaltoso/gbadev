## When to use it

For anything that moves on its own: characters, enemies, bullets, cursors. The GBA draws up to 128 sprites on top of (or between) the backgrounds, each one with its own position, flip and priority, and moving them costs almost nothing.

For big things that don't move apart from the rest, like the level itself, use a background instead.

## How to think about it

**A sprite item is data, a `bn::sprite_ptr` is a sprite on screen.** Each `.bmp` with its `.json` in `graphics/` becomes, at build time, a header `bn_sprite_items_<name>.h` with a `bn::sprite_items::<name>` object. It lives in ROM and shows nothing. `create_sprite(x, y)` copies its tiles and palette to video memory and returns a `bn::sprite_ptr`. The sprite stays on screen while some `bn::sprite_ptr` pointing to it is alive.

**`(0, 0)` is the center of the screen, and the position is the sprite's center.** x grows to the right and y grows down. The screen goes from -120 to 120 horizontally and from -80 to 80 vertically.

**Positions are `bn::fixed`, not `int`.** `bn::fixed` is a fixed-point number, so a sprite can move half a pixel per frame: the halves add up and it moves one pixel every two frames. The hardware still draws at whole pixels.

**An animation is a list of frames and a speed.** `bn::create_sprite_animate_action_forever` loops, `bn::create_sprite_animate_action_once` stops at the last frame and then `done()` returns `true`. Either way the action only advances when you call `update()`, once per frame.

**Flip is a property of the sprite.** `set_horizontal_flip(true)` mirrors the sprite in hardware, with no extra tiles. It stays until you change it, whatever animation is playing.

## Pitfalls

**"no matching function for call to `bn::sprite_ptr::sprite_ptr()`".**
`bn::sprite_ptr` has no default constructor: there is no "empty sprite". A class member of that type must be created in the constructor's initializer list. If the sprite really may not exist, use `bn::optional<bn::sprite_ptr>`, the same way the snippet does with its animation.

**The animation is stuck on its first frame.**
Either you never call `update()` on the action, or you create the action again every frame, for example inside an `if(bn::keypad::a_held())`. A new action starts at frame 0. Create it once, when the animation starts (`pressed` is usually the right moment), and update it every frame after that.

**The character faces the wrong way after changing animation.**
The flip belongs to the sprite, not to the animation, so a new animation keeps the flip of the previous one. Whenever you start an animation, set the flip too, even when it's `false`.

**A box or a halo around the sprite.**
Palette index 0 is transparent, whatever color it is. If your image editor put the background color somewhere else in the palette, the background is drawn. Semi-transparent pixels are another source: the GBA has no per-pixel alpha, so soft shadows and anti-aliased edges must become solid colors before import. That's why the snippet's tool blends the shadow onto the backdrop color.

**The importer complains about the colors or the bits per pixel.**
A 4bpp sprite has 16 colors, the transparent one included. With more colors the sprite becomes 8bpp, which takes twice the video memory, or the import fails if the `.json` asks for `"bpp_mode": "bpp_4"`. Reduce the colors in your editor, and make sure the `.bmp` is indexed and uncompressed.

**The sprite looks off-center.**
The position is the center of the frame, not of the drawing. If your art sits in a corner of a 32x32 cell, so does it on screen. Center the art in the cell, or account for the offset.
