## When to use it

Whenever the world is bigger than the screen. Without a camera you'd move every background and every sprite yourself, in the opposite direction, every time the view changes. With `bn::camera_ptr`, you move one thing.

## How to think about it

**Screen positions become world positions.** Without a camera, a sprite at `(10, 0)` is 10 pixels right of the screen's center. After `sprite.set_camera(camera)`, `(10, 0)` is a spot in the world, and Butano draws the sprite at its position minus the camera's. The same goes for backgrounds.

**The camera is the center of the screen.** A camera at `(0, 0)` shows the world from -120 to 120 horizontally and from -80 to 80 vertically. Move it right, and everything attached to it slides left on screen.

**Whatever has no camera stays on the screen.** That's how you make a HUD: hearts, score and menus simply don't call `set_camera`.

**The camera must stop half a screen before the edge.** A map centered on `(0, 0)` goes from minus half its size to plus half its size. Since the camera is the screen's center, it can go up to half the map minus half the screen: for a 512x512 map, ±136 horizontally and ±176 vertically. `bn::clamp` keeps it there.

**Following is choosing where the camera goes each frame.** Set it on the target and it follows rigidly. Keep it within a box around the target and you get a dead zone, where small moves don't scroll the screen (that's another `clamp`). Or move it a fraction of the remaining distance every frame and you get a smooth follow: fast when far, slow when close. The snippet does the last one.

## The smallest thing that works

The **D-pad** moves the camera around a 512x512 map. **A** makes it glide to the sleeping stump, wherever you are.

## Pitfalls

**Something stays glued to the screen while the world moves.**
It has no camera. Every sprite and background that belongs to the world needs `set_camera`, including the ones created later, like a bullet or an enemy that spawns mid-game.

**The other side of the map shows up at the edge.**
The camera went further than half a screen from the edge, and the background [wrapped around](/basics/backgrounds/#how-to-think-about-it). Clamp the camera after every change, whatever moved it.

**The limits are off by a factor of 8.**
`map_item().dimensions()` is in 8x8 cells. Half of a map's width in pixels is `dimensions().width() * 8 / 2`.

**The camera stutters, or behaves differently on each side of `(0, 0)`.**
`bn::clamp` needs its three arguments to have the same type. Calling `.integer()` on the position to match `int` limits throws the fraction away, so a smooth camera moves in uneven whole-pixel jumps. It also rounds toward zero, so everything from -1 to 1 becomes 0: around the center, one pixel takes twice as long as anywhere else. Make the limits `bn::fixed` instead, as the snippet does. When you do need an integer, `floor_integer()` always rounds down.
