## When to use it

Whenever something must not walk through the level: walls, water, furniture, the edge of the map. Collision between two moving things, like a player and an enemy, is a different problem: compare their boxes directly.

## How to think about it

**Collision is a second map, made of cells.** The GBA doesn't know what is solid; your game does. Keep a grid that says, for each cell, whether it blocks. It can be a list written by hand, an export from a map editor, or the tile indexes of the background itself (the Butano example does that). Cells are usually 16x16, bigger than the 8x8 hardware tiles, because that's the size of a "block" in most art.

**From a position to a cell: shift, floor, divide.** Butano positions have `(0, 0)` at the center, cells start at the top-left corner. So:

1. Add half the area's size: now `(0, 0)` is the top-left corner. With `floor_integer()`, -0.5 becomes -1, not 0.
2. Anything below 0 or past the size is outside. Check it now, in pixels: in C++, `-1 / 16` is `0`, which looks like the first cell.
3. Divide by the cell size: that's the column and the row.

**Check before moving.** Compute where the thing would be, ask whether that spot is solid, and move only if it isn't. Nothing is ever inside a wall, not even for a frame, so there is nothing to undo.

**A box, not a point.** Test the corners of a box around the thing. If the box is smaller than a cell, four corners are enough: no cell fits between them. A bigger box needs points along its sides, every cell size or less.

**One axis at a time.** Check the horizontal step, then the vertical one, separately. Against a wall, only the blocked axis stops. A character slides along the wall instead of sticking to it; a bouncing thing reflects on that axis and keeps the other.

## Pitfalls

**It walks into a wall near the top or the left, but not elsewhere.**
Negative numbers. `integer()` turns -0.5 into 0, and `-1 / 16` is 0 too, so a spot just past the top-left edge looks like the first cell. Use `floor_integer()` and check the bounds in pixels before dividing.

**Everything is off by a factor of 2, or the grid is shifted.**
The cell size is not the tile size: the hardware's 8x8 tiles have nothing to do with your 16x16 cells. And you forgot to add half the area before dividing, so `(0, 0)` became the corner instead of the center.

**The wrong cell blocks.**
With a flat array, the index is `row * columns + column`. `x * y` is not an index: column 2 of row 3 and column 3 of row 2 would be the same cell.

**It sticks to the wall when moving diagonally.**
You checked both axes in one step: the diagonal spot is blocked, so neither axis moves. Check and move one axis at a time.

**It passes through thin walls when it's fast.**
A step longer than a cell can jump over a whole cell without ever landing on it. Keep the speed below the cell size, or split a big step into smaller ones and check each.

**It goes through corners, or a cell between two corners.**
The box is bigger than a cell and you only check its four corners. Add points along the sides, no more than a cell size apart.
