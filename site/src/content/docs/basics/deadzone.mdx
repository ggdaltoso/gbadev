## When to use it

Whenever the camera follows the player around a world bigger than the screen. [Camera](/basics/camera/) puts the camera on the hero, or glides it there. That works, but every step scrolls the screen, even a small one to line up with a door, and the player sees as much behind as ahead. A dead zone lets the hero move a little without scrolling. Look-ahead shows more of where the hero is going. Platformers use both a lot, and top-down games too, like the snippet.

## How to think about it

The snippet has both halves, and **SELECT** switches between them: both, the dead zone only, and look-ahead only. The mode's name is in the corner of every capture below.

**The dead zone: the camera waits until the hero leaves a box.** The white corners mark a box 64 by 40 pixels around the screen's center. While the hero is inside it, the camera stays. When the hero pushes against a side, the camera moves just enough to keep it on that side. That's one `bn::clamp` per axis: the camera's position, kept within `zone_x` of the hero. Running, the hero stays on the box's edge, so it sees *less* ahead than with a camera on the hero: the box is behind it.

**Look-ahead: the camera follows a point ahead of the hero.** The pink cross is the focus: 48 pixels to the side the hero last walked, 28 up or down. With look-ahead alone, the camera sits on the focus, so walking right, the hero is left of the center and there's more to see ahead. The lead is kept when the hero stops, so standing still doesn't scroll the screen back.

**Each half fixes the other's problem, but they leak into each other.** The hero tapping left and right is where they differ most. With the dead zone only, the taps didn't move the camera at all. With look-ahead only, every tap flipped the focus toward the other side, and the camera followed: 422 pixels of scrolling. With both, the camera follows the focus through the box, and moved 46 pixels. Not 0: each tap starts the lead turning, and the focus leaves the box before the tap ends.

**The lead slides; it doesn't jump.** When the hero turns around, the focus has to cross from one side to the other, 96 pixels. Every frame it covers 1/16 of what's left, so the camera turns around smoothly. In the snippet, with both halves on, the fastest the camera moved on that turn was 5 pixels in a frame.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `bn::clamp(camera.x(), focus.x() - box_x, focus.x() + box_x)` | The dead zone in one line: the camera stays where it is unless that's more than `box_x` from the focus. |
| `bn::fixed box_x = use_zone ? zone_x : 0;` | Turning the dead zone off is a box of 0 by 0: the same `clamp` then puts the camera right on the focus. |
| `wanted_lead = bn::fixed_point();` | Turning look-ahead off slides the focus back onto the hero, 1/16 of the way a frame like any other lead. |
| `lead += (wanted_lead - lead) / lead_steps;` | Easing by a fraction of what's left, as in [Camera](/basics/camera/)'s smooth follow. Dividing a `bn::fixed_point` by an `int` divides both axes. |
| `world.dimensions().width() / 2` | Half the map in pixels, from the background itself, so the edges are right for any map size. |
| `bool right = corner % 2;` | The four corners from one loop: bit 0 is left or right, bit 1 top or bottom. One sprite, flipped. |
| `focus_marker.set_camera(camera);` | The cross is in the world, so it moves with the map. The corners have no camera, so they stay on the screen. |

## Pitfalls

**A lead that jumps jerks the screen.**
A test ROM that set `lead = wanted_lead` straight away, instead of sliding it, moved the camera 34 pixels in a single frame when the hero turned around, and 18 when it first started walking. The snippet's biggest step on the same keys was 5. The box absorbs part of the jump, but not the 96 pixels the focus crosses.

**The map's edges have to come last.**
A test ROM that kept the camera inside the map first, and applied the dead zone after, let the camera go past the map's right edge. Next to the lake it showed a row of trees that aren't there: the only trees on the map are on its left side, so the map repeats past its edge (worked out from the map). The snippet clamps to the edges last, so they always win. At the edge, the camera stops and the hero walks to the side of the screen.
