## When to use it

Whenever the camera follows the player around a world bigger than the screen. [Camera](/basics/camera/) puts the camera on the hero, or glides it there. That works, but every step scrolls the screen, even a small one to line up with a door, and the player sees as much behind as ahead. A dead zone lets the hero move a little without scrolling. Look-ahead shows more of where the hero is going. Platformers use both a lot, and top-down games too, like the snippet.

## How to think about it

**The camera follows a point, not the hero.** The snippet keeps a focus point, the pink cross, a little ahead of the hero: 48 pixels to the side the hero last walked, 28 up or down. Walking right, the hero sits left of the screen's center, and there's more to see ahead. The lead is kept when the hero stops, so standing still doesn't scroll the screen back.

**The camera moves only when the focus leaves the box.** The white corners mark a box 64 by 40 pixels around the screen's center. While the focus is inside it, the camera stays. When the focus pushes against a side, the camera moves just enough to keep it on that side. That's one `bn::clamp` per axis: the camera's position, kept within `zone_x` of the focus. In the snippet, the hero tapping left and right moved the camera 45 pixels in all. Without the box, the same taps moved it 422.

**The lead slides; it doesn't jump.** When the hero turns around, the focus has to cross from one side to the other, 96 pixels. Every frame it covers 1/16 of what's left, so the camera turns around smoothly. In the snippet, the fastest the camera moved on that turn was 5 pixels in a frame. Taps are short, so the lead only gets part of the way before turning back. That's why the taps above still moved the camera a little.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `bn::clamp(camera.x(), focus.x() - zone_x, focus.x() + zone_x)` | The dead zone in one line: the camera stays where it is unless that's more than `zone_x` from the focus. |
| `lead += (wanted_lead - lead) / lead_steps;` | Easing by a fraction of what's left, as in [Camera](/basics/camera/)'s smooth follow. Dividing a `bn::fixed_point` by an `int` divides both axes. |
| `world.dimensions().width() / 2` | Half the map in pixels, from the background itself, so the edges are right for any map size. |
| `bool right = corner % 2;` | The four corners from one loop: bit 0 is left or right, bit 1 top or bottom. One sprite, flipped. |
| `focus_marker.set_camera(camera);` | The cross is in the world, so it moves with the map. The corners have no camera, so they stay on the screen. |

Art: the map is from [Big maps](/basics/bigmap/), built from [Zelda-like tilesets and sprites](https://opengameart.org/content/zelda-like-tilesets-and-sprites) by ArMM1998, CC0, and so is the hero. The corners and the cross are drawn by [`snippets/tools/deadzone_assets.py`](https://github.com/ggdaltoso/gbadev/blob/main/snippets/tools/deadzone_assets.py).

## Pitfalls

**A lead that jumps jerks the screen.**
A test ROM that set `lead = wanted_lead` straight away, instead of sliding it, moved the camera 34 pixels in a single frame when the hero turned around, and 18 when it first started walking. The snippet's biggest step on the same keys was 5. The box absorbs part of the jump, but not the 96 pixels the focus crosses.

**Without a dead zone, every tap scrolls the screen.**
A test ROM with a box of 0 by 0, so the camera sat on the focus, swung the camera back and forth by 60 to 90 pixels while the hero tapped left and right. That's the pink line in the graph above: 422 pixels of scrolling, against 45 in the snippet.

**The map's edges have to come last.**
A test ROM that kept the camera inside the map first, and applied the dead zone after, let the camera go past the map's right edge. Next to the lake it showed a row of trees that aren't there: the only trees on the map are on its left side, so the map repeats past its edge (worked out from the map). The snippet clamps to the edges last, so they always win. At the edge, the camera stops and the hero walks to the side of the screen.
