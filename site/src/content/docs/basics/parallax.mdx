## When to use it

When the camera moves and the world should look deep: hills that drift slowly behind a platformer's hero, clouds behind a shoot 'em up, a city skyline behind a runner, a layer of mist over a map seen from above. Each layer is a background of its own, so the GBA's four backgrounds give you up to four depths.

When a single picture needs bands at different speeds, for example a sky whose clouds move slower the higher they are, one background is enough with an [H-Blank effect](/effects/hblank/) that changes its position line by line. This page uses one background per layer.

## How to think about it

**Each layer moves a fraction of what the camera moves.** The snippet has one camera position, and each layer is moved left by the camera times its speed: the ground by 1, the hills by 1/2, the sky by 1/8. The smaller the fraction, the farther away the layer looks, the way a mountain seems to follow you from a moving car while the fence rushes past. A fraction of 1 is a layer stuck to the world; 0 is one stuck to the screen.

**A slower layer needs less width.** The snippet's camera goes from -136 to 136: 272 pixels. The ground moves all of it, so it has to cover the screen's 240 pixels plus 272: 512. The hills move half, 136, and need 376; the sky moves 34 and needs 274. A regular background is 256 or 512 pixels wide, so here all three are 512, but the sky only just misses fitting in 256. A wider level or a slower sky changes that. Past its width, a background repeats from the other side: the art has to be drawn so that its end meets its start.

**Priorities decide what's in front, and the player has one too.** The snippet gives the sky priority 3, the hills 2 and the ground 1: a lower number is drawn in front. A sprite has a background priority as well, 3 by default, and it's drawn in front of backgrounds with the same number or higher, behind the rest. With the ground at 1, a player left at 3 would be behind the ground, so the snippet puts it at 1 too. When two backgrounds share a priority, the one created last is in front.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `constexpr bn::fixed sky_speed = 0.125;` | An eighth is exact in `bn::fixed`. The layer's position keeps the fraction, and Butano draws it at the pixel below. |
| `sky.set_x(-camera_x * sky_speed);` | The minus sign: moving the camera right moves the world left. Here both values have few decimals, so `fixed * fixed` loses nothing; see [Fixed-point numbers](/math/fixed/) for when it does. |
| `bn::fixed player_x = -200;` | The player's place in the world, kept apart from the sprite's place on the screen, which is `player_x - camera_x`. |
| `player.set_bg_priority(1);` | The sprite's priority against backgrounds, not against other sprites. That one is `set_z_order`. |
| `ground.x().integer()` | The whole pixels, for the text. `integer()` cuts toward zero, which is fine for a label. |

## Pitfalls

**The player disappears.**
In the first build of the snippet, the layers had their priorities and the player didn't: the player was nowhere on the screen, hidden behind the ground at priority 1 while it stayed at the default 3. Whenever you give the backgrounds priorities, give the sprites in front of them one too.

**Only the sky shows.**
In a test ROM with every layer left at the default priority, the layers came out right when created from the back to the front: the sky, then the hills, then the ground. Created from the front to the back, the sky was in front of everything: no hills, no ground, just the player over the sky. On a tie, the last background created wins. Set the priorities and the order stops mattering.

**The pattern breaks where the layer repeats.**
A test ROM used a sky only 256 pixels wide and scrolled it past its edge. The art repeats every 96 pixels, and 256 isn't a multiple of 96: where the background started over, two pairs of trees were 64 pixels apart instead of 96. Make a repeating layer's art repeat a whole number of times in the background's width, or make the layer wide enough that it never repeats, as the snippet does.
