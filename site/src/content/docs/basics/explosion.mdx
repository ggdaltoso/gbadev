## When to use it

When a blast should follow the corridors of a grid: the bombs of a maze game, a spell that fills the tiles in a line, a chain of crates that set each other off. The blast isn't a circle on the screen. It's a set of cells, so it can't go through walls, and whatever is on one of those cells is hit.

It builds on [Grid-locked movement](/basics/grid/): same garden, same hero walking cell by cell. Here **A** drops a bomb.

## How to think about it

**The blast is a list of cells, worked out once.** When a bomb goes off, the snippet adds a flame on the bomb's cell, then walks out along the four arms of the cross, one cell at a time, up to `range` cells. Every flame is a sprite with the cell it's on. Nothing about the blast is worked out again later: from then on, the game only reads that list.

**Each arm stops at the first wall, checked before the flame is added.** For each step out, the snippet looks at the level first. Grass: a flame goes there and the arm goes on. A bush: no flame, and `break` ends that arm at once. The cells past the bush are never even looked at, even when they're grass that the range would reach. The other arms don't care: each one has its own loop.

**Flames are cells, so whatever is on one is hit.** Is the hero in the blast? Is another bomb? The snippet answers both the same way: is there a flame whose cell is that cell? Two pairs of `int`s compared, no pixel overlap. A bomb on a burning cell has its fuse cut to one frame, so it goes off on the next frame, with its own cross: a chain. The hero is hit on the cell the rules see it on, which, as in [Grid-locked movement](/basics/grid/), is already the next cell as soon as a step starts.

## The smallest thing that works

Walk with the **D-pad** and drop a bomb with **A**, up to three at a time. Each one goes off after a second and a half, in a cross three cells long that stops at the bushes. A blast that reaches another bomb sets it off, and a hero caught in one blinks.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `constexpr int arms[4][2] = { { 1, 0 }, ... };` | An array of four arrays of two `int`s: one step per arm. `for(const auto& arm : arms)` gives one row at a time, and `arm[0]` and `arm[1]` are its x and y. |
| `bn::vector<flame, max_bombs * (1 + 4 * range)>` | The capacity is part of the type, so it must be known when compiling. Built from other `constexpr` values, it is, and it follows them if the range changes. |
| `const bn::ivector<flame>& flames` | The same vector without its capacity in the type: a function that takes a `bn::ivector` works with a `bn::vector<flame, N>` of any `N`. |
| `if(--waiting.fuse <= 0)` | `--` before the name lowers the fuse first, then compares the new value. |
| `bn::erase_if(bombs, [](const bomb& gone) { ... });` | Removes every bomb the lambda says yes to. Each one's `sprite_ptr` goes with it, and so does the sprite on the screen. |
| `(drawn.x().integer() + 112 + cell_size / 2) / cell_size` | Dividing two `int`s cuts the fraction off. Adding half a cell first turns that into rounding to the nearest cell. |

## Pitfalls

**Flames on the other side of a bush.**
A test ROM with `continue` where the snippet has `break` skipped the bush and went on along the arm. Its bomb at (5, 5) burned eleven cells instead of seven: two above the bush over the bomb, at (5, 3) and (5, 2), and two past the bush on its left, at (3, 5) and (2, 5), in corridors the blast should never reach. `continue` skips one cell; `break` ends the arm. Not measured: in a level without bushes all around its edge, an arm that doesn't stop would also read past the ends of `level`.

**The bomb lands in front of the hero.**
A test ROM that dropped the bomb on `cell_x` and `cell_y`, the hero's cell for the rules, made the drop happen on its own 2 pixels into a step to the right. The bomb showed up on the next cell, the one the hero was walking to, 14 pixels ahead of the sprite. The snippet, at the same moment, put it on the cell the hero was leaving, 2 pixels behind. The rules can see the hero in the next cell, but a bomb is drawn: drop it where the sprite is.

**"Vector is full" when two bombs go off together.**
A test ROM with room for one blast, `1 + 4 * range` flames, ran fine with one bomb. With a bomb at (5, 5), seven flames, and a second at (7, 5) set off by the chain on the next frame, eight more, the fourteenth `push_back` stopped the game on Butano's error screen. Size the vector for every bomb going off at full size: here three bombs of 13 flames, 39.
