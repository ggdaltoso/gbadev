## When to use it

When the world is bigger than a few screens and the player should know where they are in it: an overworld, a big dungeon, a town on [a big map](/basics/bigmap/). A minimap answers *where am I* and *what's around* without leaving the game, so it stays on screen while the hero walks.

## How to think about it

**A minimap is a second picture of the world, made once.** The game doesn't shrink the map while it runs. The art script does it when the art is built: one minimap pixel for each 16x16 tile of the world, colored with the tile's most common color. Averaging the colors instead smeared the houses into the grass and gave 94 colors; the most common color keeps each region flat. The 1024x1024 world becomes 64x64 pixels, the largest a single sprite can be, in 12 colors plus a dark frame.

**Both pictures are centered on (0, 0), so a position just gets divided.** The world goes from -512 to 512; the minimap, from -32 to 32 around its own center. A spot in the world divided by 16 is the same spot on the minimap, measured from the minimap's center. Add the minimap's position on screen, and that's where the marker goes. No offsets, no corners: that's the reason to keep both centered.

**Anything with a position can be a marker.** The dot is the hero's position divided by 16. The box is the camera's: the camera is the center of the screen, so its position divided by 16 is the center of what the screen shows, and the box is 15x10 pixels because 240x160 divided by 16 is 15x10. An enemy, a chest or a goal would be the same line with its own position. Markers and the minimap have no camera, so they stay put on the screen like any [HUD](/basics/hud/).

The world and the hero are the ones from [the big map page](/basics/bigmap/); what's new is the three sprites in the corner and the two lines that place the markers.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `constexpr bn::fixed_point minimap_center(bn::display::width() / 2 - 36, ...);` | A point worked out at compile time from the screen's size: 36 is half the minimap plus a 4 pixel margin from the corner. |
| `minimap_center + hero.position() / minimap_scale` | A `bn::fixed_point` divided by an `int` is still a `bn::fixed_point`, with the fraction kept. The sprite is drawn at that position rounded down, the same way on both sides of 0. |
| `dot.set_visible(frame % 32 < 24);` | A comparison is already a `bool`, so it goes straight into `set_visible`: shown for 24 frames out of 32. |

Art: [Zelda-like tilesets and sprites](https://opengameart.org/content/zelda-like-tilesets-and-sprites) by ArMM1998, CC0, through the big map page's world. The minimap, the dot and the box are made from that world by `snippets/tools/minimap_assets.py`.

## Pitfalls

**The dot sticks in the middle of the map.**
Dividing whole numbers, `hero.x().integer() / 16`, rounds toward zero, so -15 and 15 both give 0. In the test ROM, sweeping the hero from -40 to 40, the minimap column at 0 covered 31 world positions and its neighbors 16 each: the dot stood still twice as long around the center. Dividing the `bn::fixed` position keeps the fraction, and every column covered 16.

**The markers vanish.**
Creating the minimap after the dot and the box hid both: in the test ROM the minimap showed, and neither marker did. Sprites with the same z order and priority are drawn with the newest on top. Create the markers last, as the snippet does, or give them a lower `set_z_order` than the minimap.

**A sprite can't be bigger than 64x64.**
A 128x128 minimap stopped the build with *Invalid sprite width: 128*, followed by the list of sizes a sprite can have. For a bigger minimap, cut it into several sprites of 64x64, or draw it on a background of its own.
