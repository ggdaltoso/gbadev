## When to use it

In any top-down or three-quarter view, where things stand on a floor seen from above at an angle: a village, a dungeon, a market. The hero walks around a statue, and has to disappear behind it from one side and cover it from the other. Without sorting, whatever was created last is always on top.

## How to think about it

**Lower on the screen is closer.** The floor goes away from you toward the top of the screen. So two things that overlap are ordered by where they touch the floor: the one whose feet are lower is in front. Not the top of the sprite, and not its center, because things have different heights. A tall statue's center is high up, but its feet are what decide.

**A z order is that depth as a number.** Butano draws sprites with a higher `z_order` first, so they end up behind. Screen y grows downward, so the z order is the feet's y with the sign flipped: `-(y + feet)`. Things that never move get it once. The hero moves, so the hero gets it again every frame.

**A big thing is one thing.** A sprite is 64x64 at most, so the market stall is 9 sprites of 32x32. If each piece sorted by its own feet, the hero could stand between two rows of the same stall. So all 9 get the z order of the stall's ground, under its bottom row. One z order for 9 sprites is also one sort layer instead of three, which matters below.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `void stand(bn::sprite_ptr& sprite, int feet)` | A reference to the caller's sprite. A copy of a `bn::sprite_ptr` would still point at the same sprite, since it's a handle, but each copy adds and drops a reference to it. |
| `constexpr bn::fixed_rect solids[] = { ... }` | The solid bases as data, built by the compiler. Moving the statue means moving its rect too, so they sit next to each other in the code. |
| `for(const bn::fixed_rect& solid : solids)` | A range-based `for` over a plain array: the compiler knows its size. `const&` reads each rect without copying it. |
| `index % 3 * 32` and `index / 3 * 32` | One index from 0 to 8 turned into a column and a row of a 3x3 grid, the same order the art script cut the pieces in. |
| `stall.back().y() + stall_feet` | The last piece is on the bottom row, so its bottom edge is the stall's ground. |
| `-ground_y.round_integer()` | `z_order` takes an `int`. Positions here are whole pixels anyway, so rounding changes nothing, but it says which way a half pixel goes. |

## Pitfalls

**The sign is backwards.**
With `z_order = y + feet`, without the minus, everything lower on the screen goes further back. In the test ROM the hero stood in front of the statue's base, and the statue covered all of them except the feet. Higher z orders are drawn first; that's easy to read the other way.

	<figcaption>Top: `-(y + feet)`. Bottom: `y + feet`. Same spot, 2x.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Sorting by the center.**
`sprite.y()` is the center of the sprite, and the hero is 32 pixels high while the statue is 64. Sorting by the centers, the test ROM drew the hero on top of the statue while they stood behind it. Worked out from the two sizes, the order is wrong for 20 pixels of walking, from where the hero's center passes the statue's to where their feet pass its base.

<figure style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
	<img src="/images/depth/center.png" width="192" height="330" style={{ imageRendering: 'pixelated', marginInline: 'auto' }} alt="Top: the hero behind the statue, hidden by it. Bottom: the same spot sorted by the centers, with the hero drawn on top of the statue." />
	<figcaption>Top: sorted by the feet. Bottom: by the centers. Same spot, 2x.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Each piece of a big thing sorted by itself.**
Giving each of the stall's 9 pieces its own z order put the hero, standing behind the stall, in front of the awning's row and behind the two rows below it. Only their head showed, on top of the awning.

<figure style={{ textAlign: 'center' }}>
	<img src="/images/depth/pieces.png" width="192" height="330" style={{ imageRendering: 'pixelated', marginInline: 'auto' }} alt="Top: the hero behind the stall, hidden. Bottom: the same spot with each piece sorted by itself, and the hero's head shows over the awning." />
	<figcaption>Top: one z order for the stall. Bottom: one per piece. Same spot, 2x.</figcaption>
</figure>

**"No more sprite sort layers available".**
Butano groups sprites by their pair of background priority and z order, and has room for 16 of those groups. In the test ROM, 16 different pairs worked and the 17th stopped the game with that message, from `bn_sorted_sprites.h`. The snippet uses at most 6: the two flags share one, and so do the 9 pieces of the stall. A crowd where everyone moves, each at their own y, runs out fast. The 16 is `BN_CFG_SPRITES_MAX_SORT_LAYERS` in `bn_config_sprites.h`.

**Background priority beats z order.**
`set_bg_priority` puts sprites in front of or behind backgrounds, and it's checked before the z order. In the test ROM, a villager with background priority 2 and z order 1000 was drawn in front of the hero with priority 3 and z order -1000, though its z order said far behind. Keep everything that sorts by depth at the same background priority.

**Two things at the same depth.**
With equal z orders, the test ROM drew the sprite created later on top, in both of two pairs tried. The two flags here share a z order but never overlap, so it doesn't matter. If two things can stand on the same line and overlap, don't count on that order: break the tie, for example by x.
