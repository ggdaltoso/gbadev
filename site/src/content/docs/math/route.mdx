## When to use it

When a character should walk somewhere on its own, along a way you chose: a villager going about their day, a guard on patrol, a worker from the door to their desk, a cutscene walk, a moving platform. The way is known when you make the level, so there's nothing to search: you write it down as a list of points.

When the way has to be found in play, around walls that change or to a goal that moves, see [Pathfinding](/math/pathfinding/). For one move from A to B in a set time, with easing, see [Interpolation and easing](/math/easing/).

## How to think about it

**A route is a list of stops, and a stop can be a wait.** Each stop is a cell of the village and how many frames to stay there. The villager walks from one stop to the next, in order, and after the last one comes the first again, so the route loops. Waiting at the door and at the board isn't separate code: it's a number in the list. The art script reads the same list and lays the dirt path under it.

**A leg is walked by distance, not by steps toward a point.** When a leg starts, the snippet knows its length: the square root of `dx² + dy²`, once. Every frame it adds the speed to `walked`, and puts the villager `walked / length` of the way from one stop to the next, on both axes at once. The same fraction on both axes is a straight line, even on a slant, and the same pixels per frame on every leg, whatever its length.

**A leg ends when the distance is covered, and the stop is put exactly in place.** When `walked` reaches the length, the snippet doesn't move the villager by one more step: it puts it on the stop, starts the next leg from 0 and starts the stop's wait. No position is ever compared with a point, so there's nothing to overshoot or fall short of, whatever the speed. Each leg takes its length divided by the speed, rounded up, in frames: one loop of the route is {loopFrames} frames.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `constexpr waypoint route[] = { { 2, 3, 60 }, ... };` | An array of structs, each written as `{ x, y, wait }` in the order of its members. `constexpr` keeps it in ROM. |
| `constexpr int stops = sizeof(route) / sizeof(route[0]);` | The size of the whole array over the size of one element: the number of stops, which follows the list if it grows. |
| `route[(leg + 1) % stops]` | The stop after `leg`; after the last one, `%` brings it back to 0. See the pitfalls for what happens without it. |
| `int length = bn::sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy);` | The square root of an `int`. `bn::sqrt` of a `bn::fixed` keeps only 6 bits of fraction, as the [Vectors](/math/vectors/) page measured. |
| `bn::fixed_point(dx, dy) * walked / length` | Multiplies before dividing, so `walked / length`, a fraction under 1, never gets rounded on its own. |

## Pitfalls

**The villager stops just short of a stop, forever.**
A test ROM that moved the villager toward the next stop by the speed, in the direction worked out from where it was, and only went on when its position was equal to the stop, never left the first corner. It ended up less than a pixel from the stop and stayed there. Worked out from its code: that close, the square root of what was left rounds to 0, so it stopped moving without ever being equal. It stood there for the rest of the test. Measure the leg by distance and end it when the distance is covered.

**A slanted leg turns into a bend.**
A test ROM that stepped each axis on its own, at most 1.5 pixels each, like the slide in [Grid-locked movement](/basics/grid/), went across the square at 45 degrees until the 32 pixels down were used up, then straight along for the rest: a bend, not the straight line. It was also faster while both axes moved, 1.5 pixels on each. Moving by the same fraction on both axes keeps the line straight.

**After the last stop, the villager loses its way.**
A test ROM that went to `route[leg + 1]` without `% stops` walked the route once. After the last stop it read past the end of the array, and never went back to the door: it walked a few pixels near the corner, jumped back, and did it again, over and over. After the art was redrawn and the ROM built again, the code unchanged, the short walks went another way. What it reads there isn't part of the route, and can be anything.
