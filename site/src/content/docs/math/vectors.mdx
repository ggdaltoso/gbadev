## When to use it

When something moves in a direction that isn't just left, right, up or down: a hero walking diagonally, a shot aimed at the player, an enemy that walks toward you. And when you need to know whether two things are close, with a circle instead of a box.

## How to think about it

**A `bn::fixed_point` is a vector.** It's a pair of `bn::fixed`, x and y, and it means either a place or a movement. Butano gives it `+`, `-`, `*` and `/`, so `position + velocity` moves a thing one frame, and `direction * speed` turns a direction into a velocity.

**A direction is a vector of length 1.** Multiply it by a speed, and the thing moves exactly that far each frame, whatever the angle. The D-pad doesn't give you that: right is (1, 0), length 1, but right and down is (1, 1), length 1.41.

**An angle turns into a direction with sine and cosine.** For an angle in degrees, `(cos, sin)` is the direction of length 1. The sine table from [the fixed-point page](/math/fixed/) does the work.

**A direction turns into an angle with `atan2`.** `bn::degrees_atan2(y, x)` gives the angle of the vector (x, y). That's how something aims: take the difference between the target and itself, get its angle, and turn the angle back into a direction of length 1.

**Comparing distances doesn't need the distance.** The real distance is `sqrt(x² + y²)`. But if `x² + y²` is less than `r²`, the distance is less than `r`, so you can skip the square root and compare the squares.

## Pitfalls

**The hero is faster on the diagonal.**
Two directions at once are (1, 1), and the hero covered 212 pixels in 100 frames instead of 150. Multiply diagonals by 0.7071, which is 1/√2, as the snippet does.

**"Angle must be in the range [0..360]: -90".**
`bn::degrees_atan2` answers from -180 to 180, and everything above the shooter is negative. The sine table stops the game for those. Add 360 when the angle is negative, as `direction` does, or use `bn::degrees_lut_sin_and_cos_safe`.

**Things aim the wrong way up and down.**
On the screen, y grows downward, so 90° points down, not up. As long as the angle comes from `atan2` and goes back through the same sine table, it works out. Mixing in an angle from a math book, where 90° is up, flips it.

**`cannot convert 'bn::fixed_t<12>' to 'int'`.**
`bn::degrees_atan2` takes `int`s, not `bn::fixed`. Round the difference first, with `round_integer()`. The lost fraction only matters when the two points are a few pixels apart.

**Two far things count as touching.**
The squared length has to fit in a `bn::fixed`, about 524287, so it breaks past 724 pixels. At 800 pixels apart, it came out as -408576, which is less than any `r²`, so `closer_than` said yes. Check with a box first, or keep the squares in `int`.

**The square root is less precise than you expect.**
`bn::sqrt` of a `bn::fixed` keeps only 6 bits of fraction, so `bn::sqrt(bn::fixed(4.5))` gave 2.10937, not 2.1213. The snippet scales the value to a whole number first and takes the square root of the `int` instead.
