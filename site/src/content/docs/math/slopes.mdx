## When to use it

When the ground isn't only flat: hills, ramps, the sides of a valley. With square blocks a hill is a staircase, and the player hops up each step. With slopes the player walks up and down smoothly. The cells are still in a grid, as in [Forgiving jumps](/math/forgiving/): a slope is one more kind of cell, with a surface that changes inside it.

## How to think about it

**A slope cell has a height for every column.** The level has `/`, going up to the right, and `\`, going up to the left, one pixel up for each pixel across. In a `/` cell, the surface in the column `local_x` pixels from its left edge is `top + 17 - local_x`; in a `\` cell, `top + local_x`. `ground(x, y)` goes down the column from row `y` and returns the first surface it finds: the top of a block, or a slope's height in that column.

**The feet are one point, in the middle.** On flat ground, testing the corners of the box is right. On a slope it isn't: one corner is over higher ground than the other, and standing on the higher one leaves the middle of the player floating over the slope. So `ground` is asked about one column, the one under the middle of the feet, and the player's feet go exactly there.

**Look a little above the feet, and stick a little below.** Walking up, each step puts the slope's surface above the feet, so `ground` starts looking `step_up` pixels higher: 4, more than the 1.5 a step can climb. Walking down, each step leaves the surface a little below the feet, and gravity alone would let the player fall a bit and land, again and again. So a player who was on the ground and finds ground no more than `stick_down` pixels below is pulled down onto it.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `"...../###\\...."` | `\\` in a string is one `\` character: a single `\` would start an escape like `\n`. |
| `kind == '#' ? top : kind == '/' ? ... : no_ground` | Chained `?:`, one surface per kind of cell. |
| `(x - origin_x) % cell` | The remainder of the division: how many pixels into its cell column `x` is. |
| `constexpr int no_ground = 1000;` | A number far below the screen stands for "nothing", so the same `>=` tests work without a special case. |
| `bool sticks = on_ground && ...;` | `on_ground` is still last frame's value here: only a player who was standing sticks, not one falling past. |

## Pitfalls

**Walking downhill is a string of small falls.**
A test ROM without `stick_down` walked over the hill with → held. Going down, it was in the air for 12 captured frames in a row, the whole slope: "Air" on the top line, the player above the surface. A jump pressed then wouldn't happen, since jumping needs `on_ground`. The snippet, with the same keys, never read "Air".

**The player floats over slopes.**
A test ROM that took the higher ground under the two corners of the box floated 6 to 7 pixels above the slope at the middle of the feet, measured on the same frames where the snippet's feet were on it within a pixel. On the flat top the two agreed.

**The player walks through the hill.**
A test ROM that looked for ground from the feet down, with no `step_up`, walked straight into the hill at the height of the flat ground. Each step up put the surface above the feet, where the search didn't look, so it found the block under the hill instead, and the top line said "Ground" all the way across.
