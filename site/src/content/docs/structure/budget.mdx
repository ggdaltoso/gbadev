## When to use it

When the screen gets crowded: a shoot 'em up's bullet curtain, a horde of enemies, sparks on every hit. Butano has room for 128 sprites, and everything that's a sprite takes one: enemies, shots, the player, and each 32-pixel piece of text. Work out the budget before the game runs out of it in the middle of a level.

A game with a dozen things on screen never gets close to the limit and can skip this page. The [Object pools](/structure/pool/) page sizes one kind of object; this page splits the whole screen.

## How to think about it

**The 128 sprites are a budget, and every kind of thing gets its share.** The snippet adds up what it knows: 21 enemies, 1 ship and 4 sprites of text. The shots get what's left, 102, and that's the capacity of their vector. A shot that doesn't fit is never made, so the game can't ask Butano for a 129th sprite. Text counts too: in a test ROM, "Sprites: 128 of 128" took 4 sprites.

**Each line of the screen has a budget of its own, and the oldest sprites lose.** The hardware draws sprites one line at a time, with 1210 cycles per line, and a normal sprite costs one cycle per pixel of width ([GBATEK](https://problemkaputt.de/gbatek.htm#lcdobjoverview)). That's about 37 sprites 32 pixels wide on the same line. In a test ROM with 40 of them on the same 32 lines, mGBA drew 38: the 2 made first were missing, with no error. Thin sprites rarely get there: an 8-pixel shot costs 8 cycles, so 128 of them fit on one line.

**Whatever doesn't move on its own belongs in a background.** The sky behind the snippet is a regular background: zero sprites and zero cycles on any line. Built from 32x32 sprites, the clouds and hills at the bottom would take 24 sprites, 8 across and 3 down, and 256 cycles on each of their lines. That's 24 shots fewer, and a fifth of the line budget gone before the first shot crosses it. These numbers are worked out from the sizes, not measured. Scenery, walls and a HUD frame are tiles; sprites are for things that move one by one.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `#include "bn_config_sprites.h"` | Where `BN_CFG_SPRITES_MAX_ITEMS` comes from. Using the macro instead of writing 128 keeps the budget right if the project raises it. |
| `constexpr int max_shots = BN_CFG_SPRITES_MAX_ITEMS - ...;` | Worked out by the compiler, so it can be the vector's capacity. The macro is replaced by its value before compiling, like any `#define`. |
| `if(... && ! shots.full())` | The budget in one check: no room, no shot. Without it, `push_back` on a full `bn::vector` stops the game too, with "Vector is full" (`bn_vector.h`). |
| `sprite.set_vertical_flip(true);` | The same bullet art, pointing down. Flipping costs no extra tiles. |
| `bn::erase_if(shots, [](const shot& flying) { ... });` | Destroys the shots that left the screen, and with them their sprites. See [Game objects](/structure/objects/). |
| `bn::sprites::used_items_count()` | How many sprites Butano has made right now, hidden or not, the text included. |

## Pitfalls

**"No more sprite items available" in the middle of the game.**
In a test ROM with room for 200 shots and no `full()` check, holding A and B stopped the game about a second later, right after the count read 125: the next shot was sprite 129. It doesn't happen in a quiet moment, it happens at the busiest one. Give every kind of thing a capacity that adds up to 128. Hidden sprites count too: see [Object pools](/structure/pool/).

**"Too many on screen sprites" after raising the limit.**
`BN_CFG_SPRITES_MAX_ITEMS` can go past 128, because Butano doesn't send hidden or off-screen sprites to the hardware. In a test ROM built with it at 256, 200 sprites with 100 on screen ran fine. With 130 on screen, the game stopped with "Too many on screen sprites" (`bn_sprites_manager.cpp`, line 210). Raising it lets you keep more sprites made than shown, never more shown than 128.

**The enemies flicker out when the screen gets busy.**
The sprites dropped on a crowded line are the oldest ones. In the 40-sprite test ROM, the 2 that went missing were the first 2 made. In a shooter, the oldest sprites are usually the enemies, made at the start of the wave, so a row of big sprites can make an enemy vanish on those lines while the newer shots stay. There's no error to catch: count the width on the busiest line, or keep big sprites off the same lines. Measured in mGBA, not on a GBA.
