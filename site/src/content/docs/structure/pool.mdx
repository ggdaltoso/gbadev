## When to use it

When things appear and disappear all the time: shots, thrown stones, hit sparks. The [Game objects](/structure/objects/) page makes each one when it's born and destroys it when it's done. A pool makes all of them once, at the start, and reuses them. Making a sprite costs more than moving one, and a pool never makes one while the game runs.

Don't reach for it first. In this snippet one stone is thrown every 8 frames, and the pool saves about 2 ticks a frame out of 4389: nothing you could see. It pays off when dozens appear in the same frame. Measure with the [Performance](/structure/profiler/) page first. What a pool always gives you is a number known in advance: there are 8 stones, never a ninth.

## How to think about it

**A pool makes everything at the start: throwing wakes one up, and done puts it back to sleep.** The 8 stones are made before the game loop, hidden, and never destroyed. Each one knows if it's awake. A throw looks for the first one asleep, puts it at the hero's hand and shows it. When a stone has broken into dust, it hides again and is asleep, ready for the next throw. The vector is filled once and never erased from, so no stone ever moves inside it.

**Its size is one stone's life divided by the time between throws.** A stone flies for 40 frames and breaks into dust for 24: 64 frames. With A held, a stone is thrown every 8 frames. So when the first stone goes back to sleep, 64 / 8 = 8 have been thrown, and the ninth throw wakes the first one again. The snippet works the size out from those numbers, so changing one of them changes the pool.

**Waking a sprite costs less than making one, and the reset is most of it.** Making a sprite finds its tiles and palette in video memory, or copies them there when no other sprite has them, and sorts it in with the others. Waking one sets a position, a frame and a flag. Measured in a test ROM, for 32 stones at once, against a frame of 4389 ticks: making and destroying them took half a frame when they were the only stones, and less than a third when another stone already had the tiles. Waking and putting them back to sleep took 691 ticks, and setting the frame back to the stone was most of it: without `set_tiles`, 32 wakes and sleeps took 186.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `stone{ sprite, bn::fixed_point() }` | Aggregate initialization: the members in the order they're declared. `age` and `awake` aren't listed, so they keep their defaults. `step` is listed only because the compiler warns about a member left out with no default. |
| `constexpr int pool_size = life_frames / throw_frames;` | Worked out by the compiler, and usable as the vector's capacity. Integer division rounds down: if the life weren't a multiple of the time between throws, round up with `(life_frames + throw_frames - 1) / throw_frames`. |
| `bn::sprite_ptr sprite = ...; stones.push_back(stone{ sprite, ... });` | The stone gets a copy of the handle. When the loop's `sprite` goes away, the sprite stays: the stone still holds a handle to it. See [Game objects](/structure/objects/). |
| `for(stone& thrown : stones) { ... break; }` | A reference, so the wake changes the stone in the pool and not a copy. `break` stops at the first one asleep. |
| `continue;` | Skips the rest of the loop for a stone asleep, after counting it. |

## Pitfalls

**The stream has holes.**
A pool smaller than one life divided by the time between throws runs out. In a test ROM with 6 stones instead of 8, the throws at frames 48 and 56 found every stone awake and didn't happen, so 2 of every 8 throws were missing: the stones flew 16 pixels apart, with a 48-pixel hole every 8 throws. Work the size out from the timing, as the snippet does.

**A thrown stone is already dust.**
A woken stone keeps everything from its last throw, and it went to sleep on its last dust frame. In a test ROM without the `set_tiles(..., 0)` in the wake, the first 8 throws were stones, and from then on every stone flew as the few specks of dust it ended on. Set everything again when one wakes up: position, frame, way and age.

**"No more sprite items available".**
A hidden sprite is still a sprite. Butano keeps every sprite, shown or not, in a pool of its own of `BN_CFG_SPRITES_MAX_ITEMS`, 128 by default (`bn_sprites_manager.cpp`). In a test ROM, making 128 hidden sprites on top of a line of text stopped the game with that error. Count the pools' sprites with everything else on screen.

**A sleeping sprite isn't free.**
Hidden sprites are skipped when drawing, but Butano still goes through them every frame. In a test ROM with a line of text on screen, the CPU went from 4.0% to 4.8% with 100 hidden sprites, and to 5.0% with the same 100 shown. For 8 stones it's nothing. A pool of hundreds costs every frame, even when nothing is thrown.
