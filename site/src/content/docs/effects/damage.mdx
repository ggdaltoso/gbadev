## When to use it

Whenever something can hurt the player more than once: enemies that walk into the hero, spikes, shots. A hit has to be felt and seen, and it can't land again on every frame the two still overlap. Most action games answer with the same three things: a short time after a hit when nothing hurts, the hero blinking through it, and a push away from what hit. The same counter drives all three.

[Hitboxes](/math/hitboxes/) decides when two things touch, and [Hitstop](/effects/hitstop/) freezes the world on the hit. This page is about what happens to the one who got hit.

## How to think about it

**After a hit, a second in which nothing hurts.** A hit sets `invincible` to 60, and every frame takes one away. While it's above 0 the hit check doesn't even run. The same counter says what the hero looks like and whether the D-pad works: the first 4 frames are white, the first 16 are the push with the keys ignored, and the rest blink. One number, counted down, instead of a flag for each.

**The push goes away from what hit, at the same speed from any side.** The direction is the hero's position minus the log's. Its length depends on how deep the two overlapped, so the snippet divides it by that length and multiplies by `knockback_speed`, 6. Every frame the push moves the hero and then loses a fifth of its speed: 6, 4.8, 3.84 and so on, about 30 pixels in all. It's added to the position before the clamp to the hedge, so a wall stops it like it stops walking.

**The flash is a palette, the blink is the counter.** `set_fade(bn::colors::white, 1)` on the hero's palette mixes every one of its colors all the way into white, without touching a tile, and `set_fade(..., 0)` gives the colors back. The blink is `invincible % 8 < 4`, worked out again every frame. Nothing remembers whether the hero is hidden, so when the counter reaches 0 the hero is shown.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `bn::sprite_palette_ptr hero_palette = hero.palette();` | A copy of the handle, not of the colors: both point to the same palette, so a fade through it changes the hero. `palette()` itself gives a `const&` that can't be changed. |
| `knockback *= knockback_friction;` | `*=` on a `bn::fixed_point` multiplies both coordinates by the same number. |
| `bn::fixed_point(dx, dy) * knockback_speed / length` | The direction kept in whole pixels, so `bn::sqrt` of an `int` gives its length, then scaled to 6. `bn::max(..., 1)` keeps the division safe when the two are at the same spot. |
| `return { sprite, walk, velocity };` | Builds an `enemy` from its members in order. The animation is made from a copy of the sprite handle, which points to the same sprite. |
| `health > 1 ? health - 1 : max_health` | The last heart doesn't go to 0: a real game would start its game over here. |

Art: the hero, the logs, the hearts and the hedge are from [Zelda-like tilesets and sprites](https://opengameart.org/content/zelda-like-tilesets-and-sprites) by ArMM1998, CC0, laid out by [`snippets/tools/damage_assets.py`](https://github.com/ggdaltoso/gbadev/blob/main/snippets/tools/damage_assets.py). The pack has only full hearts, so the script paints the empty one from it. The font is the one from [Text and HUD](/basics/hud/), from the same pack.

## Pitfalls

**The hearts are gone in five frames.**
A test ROM with neither invincibility nor a push got the same keys as the snippet. The log walked into the hero and the hit check ran on every frame they overlapped: one heart on each of 5 frames in a row, and none left. The snippet lost one heart and was pushed away. (With the push but no invincibility, that same run also lost only one: the push separated them on the next frame. Where a push can't separate them, only the counter is left; that case wasn't measured here.)

**Flashing one log flashes them all.**
The two logs are made from the same sprite item, so Butano gives them the same palette. A test ROM that faded the first log's palette on each hit turned both logs white. Giving that log a palette of its own first, with `sprite.set_palette(bn::sprite_items::log.palette_item().create_new_palette())`, made only that one white in another test ROM. The snippet's hero has no twin, so its palette is its own.

**The hero stays hidden after blinking.**
A test ROM that blinked by flipping `set_visible(! hero.visible())` every 4 frames flipped it 15 times in the 60, an odd number. When the counter ran out the hero was hidden, and stayed hidden while it walked on. The snippet, which works the blink out from the counter, showed it.
