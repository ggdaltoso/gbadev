## When to use it

When something lands hard: a sword hits, a boulder falls, a boss stomps, the player takes damage. A few frames of shaking make the hit feel heavy without any new art. Keep it for the hits that matter: if everything shakes, nothing feels strong, and a screen that never sits still is tiring to look at.

## How to think about it

**The camera is where to look, plus the shake.** The shake is an offset added to the camera's position as the very last step, and it's never stored. `focus` follows the hero smoothly, `view` is `focus` kept inside the map, and only `camera.set_position` gets `view + offset`. Next frame, the follow starts again from `focus`, which never saw the shake. The hint text isn't on the camera, so it stays still:

**Trauma adds up and drains, and the shake is trauma squared.** Each hit adds 0.5 of trauma, up to 1, and every frame drains 1/60, so a full shake lasts one second. The shake's strength is trauma squared times 8 pixels. Squaring makes one hit small (2 pixels) and three hits in a row big (7.5 pixels), and makes the end fade out softly instead of stopping:

## The smallest thing that works

The **D-pad** walks the hero, and the camera follows. **A** swings the sword. A swing that reaches the stump shakes the screen, and swinging again before the shake ends makes it stronger.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `constexpr int max_offset = 8;` | A `bn::fixed` times an `int` is exact. A `bn::fixed` times a `bn::fixed` cuts precision on both sides first: see the pitfalls. |
| `bn::min(trauma + trauma_per_hit, bn::fixed(1))` | `bn::min` takes two values of the same type. With a plain `1`, one side is `bn::fixed` and the other `int`, and it doesn't compile: `no matching function for call to 'min(bn::fixed_t<12>, int)'`. |
| `bn::optional<bn::sprite_animate_action<4>> swing;` | An action that only exists during a swing. `if(swing)` asks whether it's there, `swing->` reaches it, and `swing.reset()` throws it away. |
| `bn::fixed reach = hero.horizontal_flip() ? -gap.x() : gap.x();` | Turns "in front of the hero" into a positive number for both directions, so one check works facing left or right. |
| `focus += (hero.position() - focus) / 8;` | The smooth follow from [the camera page](/basics/camera/): an eighth of the remaining distance each frame. |
| `bn::random shake_random;` | Its own generator, so shaking doesn't change the numbers the rest of the game draws, as on [the particles page](/effects/particles/). |

## Pitfalls

**Trauma below zero brings the shake back, bigger.**
`trauma -= trauma_drain` without the `bn::max` keeps going past 0. The shake uses trauma squared, so a negative trauma shakes as hard as a positive one. In a test ROM, one hit stopped shaking at frame 30, then started again and kept growing: at frame 200, the strength was 63.63 pixels. Keep trauma at 0 or above.

**No cap, and fast hits shake off the scale.**
Without the `bn::min`, five hits 16 frames apart pushed trauma to 1.44 in a test ROM, and the strength to 16.53 pixels, twice the 8 the game was tuned for. Cap trauma at 1, and `max_offset` stays the real maximum.

**Clamping after the shake cuts it in half at the map's edge.**
With the camera already at the edge, clamping `view + offset` throws away every move past the edge. In a test ROM with full trauma for 20 frames at the right edge, the screen moved between −7 and 0 pixels, against −7 and 6 when the shake came after the clamp. Clamp first, then add the shake. The cost is that the screen can show up to 8 pixels past the map, and a regular background wraps around there, so keep those pixels plain or leave a border in the map.

**`bn::fixed` times `bn::fixed` makes the shake stop early.**
The first version of the snippet had `constexpr bn::fixed max_offset = 8`. That multiplication drops precision on both sides, so the strength came in steps of 1/8 of a pixel, and one hit stopped shaking at frame 23 instead of 30. With an `int`, the steps are gone. [The fixed-point page](/math/fixed/) has the details.
