## When to use it

For a floor that stretches to the horizon: a racing game's track, a world map seen from an airship, a battle arena seen at an angle. The GBA has no 3D, but an affine background can be scaled and moved differently on every line of the screen, and that's enough to tilt a flat map into a floor. It builds on [Rotation and scaling](/effects/affine/), for affine backgrounds, and [H-Blank effects](/effects/hblank/), for changing something on every line.

## How to think about it

**Each line of the screen is a slice of the floor at its own distance.** Picture the eye a little above a flat floor, looking at the screen. The line just under the horizon looks almost straight ahead, and meets the floor far away. The bottom line looks down, and meets it right in front. The distance is one division: the camera's height times the distance to the screen, divided by how many lines below the horizon the line is.

**Four numbers per line, rewritten in every H-Blank.** An affine background has a matrix and a starting point. The hardware walks the map from the starting point, one step per pixel to the right. Between lines, an H-Blank effect replaces four registers: `pa` and `pc` are the step, the line's scale turned to face the camera's right, and `dx` and `dy` are where the line's leftmost pixel lands on the map. The snippet fills four arrays of 160 values every frame and hands them to Butano's H-Blank effects with `reload_values_ref()`.

**The floor costs CPU on every frame.** 160 lines, each with a division and a few multiplications, worked out again every frame because the camera moves. Both include the log line itself, about 0.7% (from [Debugging](/structure/debugging/)).

## The smallest thing that works

**Up** drives forward, **Down** backs up, **Left** and **Right** turn. The track is one 1024x1024 affine background, and the car is a sprite that stays at the bottom of the screen: the floor moves, not the car.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `bn::fixed distance = camera_height * focal / below;` | The whole trick: a line `below` lines under the horizon sees the floor this far ahead. |
| `bn::fixed forward_y = -bn::degrees_lut_sin(angle);` | Angles count counterclockwise with y up, but the map's y grows down, so y flips. |
| `bn::fixed right_x = -forward_y;` | The direction across the screen: forward turned a quarter to the right. |
| `floor.dx[line] = (...).data() >> 4;` | `bn::fixed` keeps 12 bits after the point; the register wants 8. |
| `bn::affine_bg_pa_register_hbe_ptr::create(track, floor.pa)` | Butano writes `floor.pa[line]` to the register before each line is drawn, as long as the H-Blank effect is alive. |
| `track.set_wrapping_enabled(false);` | Off the map is transparent, so the lines above the horizon show the sky color. |
| `car.set_horizontal_flip(turn > 0);` | One turning frame serves both sides, mirrored. |

Art: the car is [Coupe' Car](https://opengameart.org/content/coupe-car) by Chasersgaming, CC0, cut and the track drawn by [`snippets/tools/mode7_assets.py`](https://github.com/ggdaltoso/gbadev/blob/main/snippets/tools/mode7_assets.py).

## Pitfalls

**With wrapping on, the sky fills with grass.**
A test ROM without `set_wrapping_enabled(false)` drew the whole sky green: the lines above the horizon point at -4096, and with wrapping the map repeats out there too. Near the horizon, the repeated track showed as a band of noise. Turn wrapping off, or point the sky lines somewhere transparent.

**Without `reload_values_ref()`, the floor freezes.**
A test ROM that updated the arrays every frame but never called `reload_values_ref()` showed the first frame forever: the car drove and turned, and the floor didn't move. Butano copies the values when told to, not every frame (worked out from what was seen).

**Forget that the map's y grows down, and left turns right.**
A test ROM with `forward_y = bn::degrees_lut_sin(angle)`, without the minus, looked right going straight. But with the same keys as the snippet, left turned the car the other way on the map, and it ended on the grass outside the track, where the snippet was still on the road.
