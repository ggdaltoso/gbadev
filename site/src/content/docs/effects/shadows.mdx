## When to use it

When a sprite should look like it stands on the ground and not float over it. A shadow under the feet ties it to the floor; a reflection in water says the water is there. Both cost a sprite each, and the hardware does the see-through part with [blending](/effects/blending/).

## How to think about it

**The ground is where the feet are.** The hero sprite is 32x32, but the hero touches the ground at the bottom of its boots, 10 pixels below the sprite's center. The shadow goes there, centered under the boots. It's a dark oval with blending on, so the grass shows through it and the oval only darkens it. And it's behind the hero, so it never covers the boots.

**A reflection is the hero upside down, mirrored on the feet.** A second sprite with the same tiles, flipped vertically. Water mirrors on the line where the hero touches it, so every pixel of the reflection is as far below the feet as the matching pixel of the hero is above them. The sprite's center is 10 pixels above the feet, so the reflection's center goes 10 pixels below them: 20 below the hero's. Giving the reflection the same steps as the hero keeps it on the same frame and facing the same way.

**The ground hides the reflection: a layer with a hole in it.** The reflection must show only over water. Instead of cutting the sprite, the snippet stacks the screen so it gets cut for free. The water is a background at the back. The ground is a background in front of it, with a hole where the pond is. The reflection's priority puts it between them, so the ground covers all of it except what's over the hole. The hero and its shadow are in front of everything.

Some of the C++ in the snippet:

| In the code | Why |
|---|---|
| `constexpr bn::fixed_rect pond(bn::fixed_point(-1, 24), bn::fixed_size(88, 56));` | A rectangle made from its center and its size. `constexpr`, so it's worked out by the compiler. |
| `pond.intersects(bn::fixed_rect(...))` | The feet as a small box, built on the spot and thrown away: a temporary, with no name. |
| `hero_walk reflection_walk;` | A second walker for the reflection. Each one keeps its own frame and step count, so they stay together only because they get the same steps. |
| `reflection.set_vertical_flip(true);` | Set once: `hero_walk` only sets the horizontal flip, so the vertical one stays. |
| `bn::blending::set_transparency_alpha(0.5);` | One value for the whole screen: the shadow and the reflection can't have different alphas. See [Blending](/effects/blending/). |

## Pitfalls

**The shadow erases the feet.**
A shadow made after the hero, with the same z order, is drawn in front of it: on a tie, the newest sprite is on top. It doesn't darken the boots, it replaces them: a sprite that blends mixes with the background under it, never with another sprite. In the test ROM, 20 pixels around the boots changed: where the shadow overlaps them, the boots were replaced by darkened grass. Give the shadow a higher z order, as the snippet does.

**The reflection shows on the grass.**
With the reflection at the hero's priority, nothing is in front of it, so it's drawn wherever it is. In the test ROM, with the hero away from the pond, the upside-down hero showed on the grass under its feet. Put it at the water's priority, behind the ground.

**A gap between the feet and the reflection.**
Mirroring on the sprite's bottom edge, 16 pixels below its center, instead of on the feet puts the reflection 12 pixels lower. In the test ROM, 8 rows of open water showed between the shore and the reflection's boots, so the hero seemed to float. Mirror on the row where the feet end, measured in the art.

**A shadow drawn into the art.**
The guide's shared hero has its shadow drawn into the sprite, in one opaque color picked for a plain backdrop: the GBA has no see-through pixels inside a sprite. With it, in the test ROM, 28 pixels at the shore were that flat green instead of darkened grass, with no grass showing through. The snippet uses `bare_hero`, the same frames with that color made transparent by its art script, and `hero_walk` takes the sheet to use.

**The hero disappears.**
Sprites and backgrounds both start at priority 3. Raise the ground to priority 1 and forget the hero, and the hero is behind the ground. In the test ROM, only its shadow and its reflection were left on screen. Every sprite that stands on the ground needs the ground's priority.
